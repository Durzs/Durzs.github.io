# EcoCycle — collector system explainer

## Narration script (English)

Total run time **5:04**. Timecodes are absolute, from the start of the video.


### 0:00 — Opening · Opening

- **0:04** — Four minutes. Six steps. One litre of used cooking oil.

### 0:09 — The problem · Where the oil goes today

- **0:09** — In the Pavuna–Meriti basin, thousands of small kitchens fry food every day.
- **0:14** — When the oil is used up, there is no one to sell it to — so it goes down the drain.
- **0:20** — A single litre can contaminate up to 25,000 litres of water.
- **0:26** — Inside the pipes it cools, hardens and traps everything else: a fatberg.

### 0:33 — The problem · And then the rain comes

- **0:33** — A blocked drain has nowhere to send the rain.
- **0:38** — About 13,000 unblocking operations a year in the Baixada Fluminense.
- **0:43** — The water rises in the same low-income streets, every season.

### 0:48 — The problem · The value that flows past

- **0:48** — Here is the strange part: that oil is worth money.
- **0:53** — The catadores who already work these streets live on cardboard, at R$0.15–0.40 a kilo.
- **0:59** — The oil going past them sells for R$0.80–2.50 a litre — and demand for it grew over 700% in a decade.

### 1:07 — The idea · The missing layer

- **1:08** — The workers exist. The buyers exist.
- **1:13** — What is missing is the layer in between — something that collects the oil and proves what it is.
- **1:20** — That layer is the collector system. It runs in six steps.

### 1:26 — Step 1 of 6 · Register, and get a bottle with a code

- **1:26** — Step one. A small restaurant, bar or snack shop signs up at the residents' association.
- **1:32** — It gets a clean PET bottle with a code of its own — and picks a fixed collection day.
- **1:38** — Nothing to buy, nothing to pay. The bottle is the account.
- **1:43** — Households on the same street can join too, and earn Green Points at local shops.

### 1:50 — Step 2 of 6 · The catador collects, and scans

- **1:50** — Step two. A catador from the local cooperative walks the route.
- **1:55** — At the door they check the oil through the clear bottle, and scan the code.
- **2:01** — That one scan records four things: how much, from where, when, and who collected it.
- **2:08** — The oil is no longer anonymous waste. It has a history.
- **2:14** — The shop keeps a receipt for it.

### 2:18 — Step 3 of 6 · Pooled into one verified batch

- **2:18** — Step three. Everything goes to a neighbourhood hub, hosted by the residents' association.
- **2:24** — Small pickups are pooled into a single batch, and the oil is tested for water and dirt.
- **2:30** — The batch can only ship if the total matches the sum of every scan.
- **2:36** — That is what stops oil from quietly disappearing on the way.
- **2:42** — Twenty-four small deliveries, one contract-grade batch.

### 2:46 — Step 4 of 6 · Sold under contract — to several buyers

- **2:46** — Step four. The batch is sold under contract, to several processors — never just one.
- **2:52** — Rio learned this the hard way: the old PROVE programme depended on a single refinery.
- **3:00** — When it was shut down in 2026, the collected oil had nowhere to go.
- **3:06** — More than one buyer keeps the system alive.

### 3:10 — Step 5 of 6 · The money comes back, by a fixed rule

- **3:10** — Step five. The money comes back to the same streets, by a rule fixed in advance.
- **3:15** — Forty percent goes to the catadores who collected it.
- **3:20** — Twenty percent to households that separate their oil, twenty percent as Green Points spent in local shops,
- **3:27** — and twenty percent to the Faetequinho course, which already trades free classes for used oil in Jardim América.
- **3:35** — In the pilot that is R$113,517 a year, going back where the oil came from.

### 3:40 — Step 6 of 6 · Every scan makes the next round cheaper

- **3:40** — Step six. All of those scans are data, and the data feeds back.
- **3:45** — The system forecasts how much oil each zone will produce next month,
- **3:50** — redraws the routes so collectors walk less and carry more,
- **3:56** — and flags the shops that are about to drop out, before they do.
- **4:02** — Two dashboards share what it sees: one for the city, one for buyers looking for verified supply.

### 4:10 — The whole cycle · One litre, end to end

- **4:10** — So, the whole cycle:
- **4:14** — Kitchen. Scan. Hub. Contract. Payment. And data that makes the next round cheaper.
- **4:22** — The same litre that used to harden inside a pipe now pays a wage.

### 4:30 — What it adds up to · The pilot, in numbers

- **4:30** — In the Jardim América pilot: 75,678 litres recovered a year.
- **4:35** — R$113,517 of revenue against R$105,000 of cost — the pilot pays for itself.
- **4:42** — R$1.27 of social value for every R$1 invested, and around 91 tonnes of CO₂ avoided.

### 4:51 — Close · Close

- **4:51** — Same oil. A different path.
- **4:56** — EcoCycle — a collection system for the last mile.
